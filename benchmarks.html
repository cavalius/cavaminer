CavaMiner benchmarks

GPUs (PearlHash) measured on 2026-11-13 with CavaMiner 0.36.0 (a development build; its kernels are those of 0.37); CPUs (RandomX) on 2026-10-18.

How it was measured

  • GPUs: a tuning run of the miner while mining on a pool: cavaminer --tune hashrate --tune-kernel search --tune-profiles default, with --tune-offset search for the undervolted rows (measured with the earlier form of that run). It measures the kernels the GPU can run, the memory clocks, power limits from the highest allowed down to the lowest the GPU holds and GPU clock locks below that, each point for 30 s after 15 s to settle. Hashrate as the pool counts it (TH/s = 10¹² multiply-accumulates per second); power is the board power from the driver's energy counter (NVML), fans included. Fans under the driver's control unless a note says otherwise.
  • Settings: Stock is the card as it comes: default power limit and clocks, the miner's default kernel. Max hashrate is the fastest point measured; balanced the most efficient point that keeps at least 90 % of that hashrate; best efficiency the most hashes per joule. The miner saves the three as the profiles max, balanced and efficient.
  • Undervolted rows add a clock offset (each clock runs at the voltage of a lower one), found by the run's offset search with self-checks and held under a clock cap. Every card is different: treat them as an example of what the search finds, not as settings to copy.
  • CPUs: cavaminer rx-bench 60 (60 s after the dataset is built), default threads, huge pages, MSR tweak; power is the CPU package power (RAPL). Lower clocks through the maximum CPU clock (--rx-max-freq) or with boost off; the clock is the mean over all cores mid-run. Balanced and best efficiency as for the GPUs.
  • Hashrates are before the dev fee (1 % PearlHash, 1 % RandomX).

Notes

  • Fans at 100 % instead of the driver's curve add up to 1 % of hashrate: the two RTX 5090s with their undervolted maximum settings went from 422.0 / 430.8 to 425.8 / 433.5 TH/s (65 / 56 °C instead of 74 / 63 °C; the warmer card drew 10 W less), the RTX 3090 from 123.5 to 124.8 TH/s (70 instead of 76 °C), the RTX 3070 at its 250 W limit from 83.7 to 84.2 TH/s (49 instead of 63 °C). 85 % gave 0.8 % on the RTX 5090s.
  • The two RTX 5090s mining side by side with their undervolted maximum settings (power limits at 600 W, fans at 100 %, clock caps 2665 / 2721 MHz) do 859.9 TH/s at 867 W together. With the offsets the cards stay under 470 W whatever the limit: their clock stops rising at about 2650 / 2700 MHz.
  • The memory clock decides more than expected at low power: the RTX 3090 at 185 W does 74.6 TH/s with 5001 MHz memory and 99.4 TH/s with 810 MHz; the RTX 3070 at 100 W 49.7 TH/s with the default clock and 55.2 TH/s with 5001 MHz. The RTX 5090's default is 13801 MHz; locked at its 14001 MHz it does the same or more for 20 to 30 W less, which is why its maximum rows are not at the highest power limit.
  • The clock caps in the RTX 5090s' undervolted rows are the ones their runs measured under (2 % above the clock of the offset search's confirmation). The miner now caps at the highest clock the search saw pass (RTX 5090: 2665 / 2721 MHz for the maximum rows; the RTX 3070's rows were measured that way).
  • The RTX 3090 does not hold power limits below 155 to 180 W with its memory at 5001 MHz or more (the driver drops it to 210 MHz, 17.5 TH/s at 103 W); with 810 MHz memory it holds its 100 W.
  • Undervolting pays most at the low end: the RTX 3070's best efficiency goes from 672 GH/W at 38 TH/s (stock curve, 57 W) to 723 GH/W at 56 TH/s (77 W).
